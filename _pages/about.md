I am a first-year CS PhD student at [Stanford University](https://www.stanford.edu/). Before Stanford, I was a PhD student at [UC San Diego](https://ucsd.edu/), advised by [Hao Su](https://www.haosu.ai/) and [Henrik I. Christensen](https://hichristensen.com/). I also led research on cross-embodiment transfer at [Physical Intelligence](https://www.physicalintelligence.company/) and worked on mobile manipulation at the [Boston Dynamics AI Institute](https://rai-inst.com/). Previously, I was a visiting student at the [Stanford Vision and Learning Lab](https://svl.stanford.edu/), working on world models for robotics with [Yunzhu Li](https://yunzhuli.github.io/) and [Jiajun Wu](https://jiajunwu.com/). I received my Bachelor’s degree in Computer Science and Statistics with the highest distinction from the [National University of Singapore](https://nus.edu.sg/), advised by [David Hsu](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~dyhsu/).

Embodied experience shapes physical knowledge and skills, yet differences in embodiment fragment that experience across agents and hardware generations. My recent work asks how learning can bridge these differences, allowing knowledge acquired through one body to benefit another. More broadly, I see general embodied intelligence as collective in both learning and behavior: agents share knowledge across embodiments and generations, collaborate with one another and with humans, and co-evolve their physical forms and capabilities over time. I am excited by the prospect that such collective robot intelligence can extend human capability and deepen our understanding of intelligence, including our own.
